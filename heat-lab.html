About this model

Fourier's law for a flat plate: q = λ ΔT / X, so ΔT = q X / λ with q = P / A. The numbers follow the lecture's worked example (Ashby, Materials Selection in Mechanical Design 6e, Exercise E2.22 and §5.18): 10 W on 1 mm², 2 mm plate, back face at 30 °C.

Conductivities: copper, aluminum and polystyrene from Callister & Rethwisch 10e, Table 19.1; AlN 170 and alumina 33 W/m·K are mid-range values from Ashby Table A.6 (140–200 and 26–39); glass 1.1 W/m·K from Ashby p. 37.

Steady or still changing?

The outside of a wall changes suddenly. If the heat flow has settled (steady), conductivity λ decides how much leaks through. While the temperatures are still changing, diffusivity a = λ/ρCp decides how fast the change creeps across: the front reaches about √(2at).

About this model

A flat wall starts at 20 °C. At t = 0 its outside face jumps to the new temperature and stays there. Sealed box: nothing removes heat from the inside face (the contents are small), so the inside drifts towards the outside temperature. Held at 20 °C: a heater or cooler keeps the inside face at 20 °C, and the plot shows the heat it must remove per square metre.

The temperatures are exact solutions of the one-dimensional heat equation (Fourier series, with image sums at short times), not a rough animation. "Halfway" is the moment the inside has moved half the way (sealed), or the leak has reached half its steady value (held).

The estimate t ≈ w²/2a (Ashby, MSMD 6e, Eq. 2.15) is an order-of-magnitude guide: the exact halfway time is about 0.76 w²/2a for a sealed wall and 0.28 w²/2a for a held one. Both grow with the thickness squared.

About this model

The curve is Parker's exact solution for an instantaneous pulse on one face of a disc that loses no heat (W. J. Parker et al., J. Appl. Phys. 32, 1679, 1961): the back face reaches half its final rise at t½ = 1.388 L²/(π² a) = 0.139 L²/a. A little detector noise is added so the reading feels like data.

Real instruments correct for heat lost from the faces and for the pulse length; this model leaves them out. Property data: alumina, AlN and copper as on the Chip bench; glass from Ashby p. 37; Mystery X from Callister Table 19.1 and Appendix B.
